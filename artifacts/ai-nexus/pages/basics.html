AI Basics

What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the field of computer science focused on building systems that can perform tasks normally associated with human thinking — like learning, reasoning, perception, and language.

A simple definition

At its core, AI is software that improves with experience or data. Instead of being told exactly what to do, an AI system is given examples or rules and learns to make decisions on its own.

Three ways to think about AI

AI is sometimes described in three useful slices: by capability, by approach, and by application. Capability tells you what it can do; approach tells you how it works; application tells you where it's used.

Types of AI

Most modern AI fits into a small number of broad categories. Two of the most common are based on how general the system is:

  • Narrow AI — designed for a single task, like spam filtering, image tagging, or playing chess. All AI used in products today is narrow AI.
  • General AI — a hypothetical system that can perform any intellectual task a human can. It does not yet exist.
  • Super AI — a theoretical level beyond human ability. This is currently a topic of research and speculation, not engineering.

How AI learns

Most modern AI learns from data. You feed it examples (pictures of cats and dogs, customer reviews, weather records), and it discovers patterns it can apply to new examples it has never seen before. This is called machine learning, and it is the engine behind nearly every AI product you use.

What AI is not

AI is not magic, and it is not conscious. It does not "understand" the world the way you do. It is, in essence, very good pattern recognition combined with very fast math.
